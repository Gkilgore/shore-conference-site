About the conference

The Shore Conference is the athletic conference for 48 high schools in Monmouth and Ocean counties, public and private.

Teams are organized into divisions by sport. The conference runs its own tournaments. State tournaments are run by the NJSIAA.

1936 to 1969

History of the Shore Conference

Nine schools met in February 1936 to form a league for the smaller public high schools of Monmouth and Ocean counties. By the end of the 1960s it had 27 members, four divisions and leagues from bowling to wrestling.

From the conference’s own record of its meetings, posted in 2022. The record stops in 1969.

1936–19491950s1960sOfficers
1936–1949

A league of Group Two schools

The conference began as a way for the smaller public high schools of Monmouth and Ocean counties to play for titles in football, basketball, baseball and track. Within ten years it had a membership cap, a waiting list and a basketball tournament that paid for itself.

February 1936

The Shore Conference of Group Two Public Schools is organized, with plans for member schools to compete for titles in football, basketball, baseball and track.

Roy W. H. Maurer, athletic director at Freehold High School and the motivating force behind the organization, is elected president unanimously. Nine of the eleven Group Two schools in the two counties send delegates or say they intend to join. Matawan and Leonardo are not represented.

William Woolley of Neptune is vice president and C. S. Bergen of Point Pleasant is secretary and treasurer. Abe Smith of Manasquan and Arnold Truex of Atlantic Highlands join them on the Executive Council.

  • 9schools at the founding meeting in February 1936
  • 12schools allowed under the 1940 membership cap
  • $980cleared by the 1946 basketball tournament, a record at the time

December 1940

Sayreville High School’s application is rejected after a rule is adopted limiting membership to twelve schools: Rumson, Leonardo, Lakewood, Manasquan, Matawan, Atlantic Highlands, Point Pleasant, Toms River, Keyport, Freehold, Neptune and Hoffman of South Amboy.

A rule barring fifth-year students from competing is rejected, since a 19-year-old age limit is already in effect.

June 1945

The conference drops the weighted point system used to decide championships. Titles in the 13-team league will go by won-loss percentage.

April 1946

The baseball championship will be scored under the point system used for basketball. The conference is again split into first and second divisions, as in basketball, but with no playoffs.

The basketball tournament reports that it cleared $980, the largest total it has ever received.

October 1946

The conference refuses membership to Red Bank Catholic, saying it will abide by a constitution that makes only public schools eligible.

1950–1959

Divisions, records and new members

Growing enrollments forced the conference to sort its schools by size, first as Group I and Group II and then as A and B divisions. Membership reached sixteen, then nineteen, and the record book filled up.

February 1951

The 13 members vote unanimously to adopt a football scheduling plan like the one used for baseball and basketball, starting in 1952 as a two-year pilot. The five Group I and seven Group II schools will play in two divisions for separate trophies. Hoffman is the only member without football.

Titles go by points: two for a win, one for a tie. If teams tie for first, two trophies are awarded. The conference will keep competing for the Rutgers Trophy, decided by the Dickerson rating system, until a school retires it with three championships.

Red Bank is declared basketball champion on its undefeated regular season. The playoffs are dropped in favor of a four-team invitational tournament.

September 1951

The basketball playoffs change again. Six teams qualify, three from each division. The second- and third-place teams play for the right to meet the division’s top team, and each division crowns its own champion.

February 1952

Johnny Coil of Hoffman breaks two all-time Shore scholastic records, scoring 65 points in one game on 26 field goals. Jim Hudson of Atlantic Highlands had scored 56.

Sayreville asks again to join. The Executive Committee denies the request for now, pending further study.

December 1952

With enrollments rising, the Group I and II divisions become A and B, the larger schools in A. Projections show only three Group I schools left next year: Rumson, Atlantic Highlands and Hoffman.

The Rutgers Cup is presented to Leonardo for its third football title, after wins in 1946 and 1948. Granny Magee of Manasquan, the conference’s NJSIAA representative, will again run the basketball tournament at Convention Hall in Asbury Park.

February 1953

The conference adopts the Rosati Plan, submitted by Rumson athletic director Joe Rosati. Each school plays a home-and-home series within its division, and the divisions meet only in golf and bowling. Division A: Red Bank, Middletown, Neptune, Freehold, Manasquan, Toms River and Lakewood. Division B: Rumson, Atlantic Highlands, Matawan, Keyport, Point Pleasant and Hoffman. Opponents of the plan fear it could split the conference into two leagues.

Jim Hudson of Atlantic Highlands becomes the first player to score 300 points in conference competition, passing John Coil’s 283.

April 1954

Granville Magee, principal of Manasquan High School, asks to be relieved of his duties as basketball tournament director.

October 1955

Freehold High School is suspended for the 1955–56 school year for using an ineligible player and is asked to return its Class A championship trophies from 1953 and 1954.

The conference then reverses the suspension, accepting the argument that the newly organized Freehold Regional district should not be penalized for mistakes made before regionalization.

December 1955 to March 1956

Asbury Park, Long Branch, Central Regional and Red Bank Catholic apply for membership. Asbury Park and Central Regional are accepted in February. Red Bank Catholic is not admitted, because the by-laws limit membership to public high schools.

Long Branch is admitted in March, bringing membership to 16 schools.

June 1956

The Executive Committee will have five members: the past president, one coach from each division and two school administrators.

October 1956

Don Lewis of Freehold (1953) and Avon “Bobo” Reeves of Long Branch (1956) hold the post-war record for points in a football season with 120. George Ruth of Keyport scored 118 in 1956, Ted Laures of Middletown 115 in 1948 and Ed Weber of Matawan 100 in 1947.

December 1956

Cross country is added. Charles Nelson, Freehold athletic director, will coordinate the sport with Asbury Park, Red Bank, Long Branch, Freehold, Central and Lakewood. The first conference meet is set for Ocean County Park on November 5, 1957.

  • 65points in one game by Johnny Coil of Hoffman, February 1952
  • 2,003career points for Jack Arden of Lakewood, the first in New Jersey past 2,000
  • 57straight golf wins for Lakewood before Manasquan ended the run in 1958
  • 16member schools once Long Branch joined in March 1956

February 1957

Jack Arden of Lakewood sets the conference’s season basketball scoring record with 412 points.

December 1957

Wall Township, Southern Regional and Brick Township are admitted. Southern and Brick will compete in 1958, Wall in 1959.

Bowling expands to 14 teams at Elm Lanes in Eatontown. Seven schools agree to play Tuesday basketball games in the afternoon; a school that refuses an afternoon game forfeits. Dues rise from $2 to $10 a season.

February 1958

Jack Arden breaks the state basketball scoring record of 1,737 points and becomes the first player in New Jersey history to score more than 2,000 in a career, finishing with 2,003.

The conference will split into North and South divisions, with placement reviewed once male enrollment figures for 1958–1961 are in. It votes to name its own All-Conference teams in football, basketball and baseball, and approves a seven-team golf schedule.

May 1958

Lakewood’s streak of 57 straight golf match wins ends with a loss to Manasquan.

December 1958

A realignment for 1960 is presented. Group A: Asbury Park, Freehold, Long Branch, Middletown, Neptune and Red Bank. Group B North: Rumson, Keyport, Matawan, Manasquan, Wall, Hoffman and Atlantic Highlands. Group B South: Brick, Central, Lakewood, Point Pleasant and Southern.

Bowling divisions are set up for the first time. Two representatives of Red Bank Catholic attend to ask for entry; the request goes to the Executive Committee.

December 1959

Schedules for 1960–61 are approved. The Group 4 schools, Freehold, Asbury Park, Middletown and Long Branch, worry that Group 2 and 3 schools are dropping them from their schedules.

1960–1969

Realignment, new sports and 27 schools

Scheduling and grouping dominated the decade. Schools threatened to leave, some did, and one came back. Soccer, wrestling, tennis and gymnastics arrived, and by 1969 the conference had settled on four divisions for 27 schools.

March 1960

A realignment and scheduling proposal would reassess divisions every two years by counting boys in grades 10 to 12. Officials’ fees are made uniform: $12.50 for basketball, $17.50 for football and baseball.

Tighter rules on athletes’ behavior are proposed.

Reportedly, members of bowling teams were smoking during matches and golf team members were wagering on their games.

From the conference record, March 1960

April to June 1960

The conference votes 15–3 for a two-division A and B alignment starting in 1962–63, with enrollment checked every two years. Asbury Park and Lakewood say they intend to withdraw.

By June, Asbury Park confirms it will leave. Lakewood reconsiders and stays. Keyport, which had also talked of leaving, remains.

December 1960

Scheduling and grouping are still the main business. Hoffman indicates it may leave, which would affect the alignment.

March 1961

Basketball and baseball schedules for 1962–63 are approved. Most schools want a nine-game football schedule. Soccer is adopted, with unofficial schedules this year and a first official schedule in 1962.

September 1961

Dues rise from $10 to $15 a school; the conference has been operating in the red for several years. Individual medals will be awarded only in golf, cross country and track. In other sports, trophies go to the champion schools.

Shore Regional and Monmouth Regional are added. Shore will arrange its own schedules until 1964. Hoffman and Asbury Park will drop out after the 1961–62 school year.

March 1962

Wrestling becomes an official sport. Len Cooke, Wall athletic director, presents a wrestling schedule, which is approved.

June 1962

Jackson Township asks to be admitted. The conference now has 20 member schools.

February 1963

Football schedules for 1964 are approved with a new A, B and C alignment. Schools must now apply separately to the wrestling, soccer, golf and cross country leagues.

Bob Verga of St. Rose averages 36.8 points a game and passes the Shore single-season mark of 750 points, set by Jack Arden in 1958. Verga sets Shore and state single-season records with 1,033 points.

May 1963

A tennis league is added for 1964, leaving the Central Jersey Tennis League, in existence since 1942, with four non-conference schools. Ten teams will play in two soccer divisions.

Because of the conference’s growth, the post of executive secretary is created, open to male faculty of member schools, with a $200 stipend and $75 more for clerical help.

September 1963

Cross country championships will be run separately for the A and B divisions rather than as one meet. The conference now has 23 members in two divisions.

February 1964

Schedules for 1964–65 and 1965–66 are approved, including the first tennis league schedule. The basketball tournament nets $2,298.

The Executive Committee proposes a 6:30 p.m. start or six-minute quarters for JV basketball to speed up the night. Other ideas: moving baseball a week later into June for better weather, and a list of basketball officials to guide hiring.

June to September 1964

Arnold Truex of Middletown, a charter member since 1936, begins his third term as president. The conference sets up a basketball officials assignment committee and a budget committee, and warns coaches and athletic directors against illegal scouting of football games.

February 1965

A new grouping plan goes to school officials for study. The conference is a large-school A Division with the smaller schools in north and south B divisions; the plan calls for A, B and C groups by enrollment in the top three grades.

No freshman aged 16 may compete in freshman sports. A two-mile run is added to track meets as a non-scoring event.

  • 1,033points in one season by Bob Verga of St. Rose, a Shore and state record in 1963
  • $2,298netted by the 1964 basketball tournament
  • 23–1vote for four divisions in March 1969
  • 27schools in the alignment for 1970–71

March to May 1965

The new grouping for 1966–67 passes 15–2. Rumson-Fair Haven protests, citing more travel and the loss of traditional rivals, and in May announces it will withdraw.

The conference considers the new gym at Monmouth College as a backup for the basketball tournament if Convention Hall is unavailable.

September 1965

Matawan athletic director Barry Rizzo asks what the conference plans for girls’ sports. Five schools with girls’ programs are already competing with one another.

South Amboy High School, formerly Harold Hoffman, is denied re-entry because it is not a Monmouth or Ocean county school.

May 1966

Asbury Park is accepted back. Teams tied for first will both be declared league champions, with no playoff. A gymnastics league schedule is proposed for 1967–68.

March 1967

A realignment for 24 schools is presented for 1968–69 and 1969–70. An all-conference track meet is approved. Northern Freehold Regional is approved to join in 1968–69. Keansburg’s application for its proposed new high school is turned down as too early.

September to December 1967

Home teams in soccer will wear white and visitors dark. John Kolibas, principal of Shore Regional, suggests a sportsmanship award for member schools.

March 1968

Baseball adopts a point system: two for a win, one for a tie. A rating system for basketball officials will send the top officials to playoff games and key regular-season games.

June 1968

The conference honors past presidents Frank Pingatore and Tom Phipps of Red Bank High School, who are retiring.

February to May 1969

An assignor for basketball officials is approved. A four-division alignment, A, B, C and D, passes 23–1 with Wall dissenting. It is built on 27 schools, chosen over a 9-9-9 structure because it leaves more room in scheduling.

Schedules for 1970–71 and 1971–72 are approved, and three new schools officially enter: a new Toms River high school, Marlboro and Keansburg.
