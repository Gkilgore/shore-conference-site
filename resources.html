About the conference

The Shore Conference is the high school athletic conference for public and private schools in Monmouth and Ocean counties, New Jersey.

Teams play in divisions that are set sport by sport. The conference runs its own tournaments, and the NJSIAA runs the state tournaments.

1936 through 1969

History of the Shore Conference

Nine schools met in February 1936 to form a league for the smaller public high schools of Monmouth and Ocean counties. By 1969 the conference had four divisions built for 27 schools.

1936–1949

A league of Group Two schools

The conference began as a league for the smaller public high schools of the two counties, with titles in football, basketball, baseball and track.

  1. February 1936

    The Shore Conference of Group Two Public Schools is organized. Nine of the eleven Group Two schools in the two counties send delegates or say they will join. Roy W. H. Maurer, athletic director at Freehold High School, is elected president unanimously.

  2. December 1940

    Membership is limited to twelve schools, and Sayreville’s application is rejected.

  3. June 1945

    The conference drops its weighted point system. Titles in the 13-team league will go by won-loss percentage.

  4. April 1946

    The basketball tournament clears $980, the most it has ever taken in.

  5. October 1946

    Red Bank Catholic is refused membership because the constitution admits only public schools.

1950–1959

Divisions, records and new members

Rising enrollments led the conference to sort its schools by size. Membership reached 16 in 1956, and three more schools were admitted the next year.

  1. February 1951

    The 13 members vote unanimously to play football in two divisions for separate trophies, starting in 1952.

  2. December 1952

    The Group I and II divisions become A and B, with the larger schools in A. Granny Magee of Manasquan will again run the basketball tournament at Convention Hall in Asbury Park.

  3. February 1953

    The conference adopts the Rosati Plan, submitted by Rumson athletic director Joe Rosati. Each school plays a home-and-home series within its division.

  4. March 1956

    Long Branch is admitted a month after Asbury Park and Central Regional, bringing membership to 16 schools.

  5. December 1956

    Cross country is added. The first conference meet is set for Ocean County Park on November 5, 1957.

  6. December 1957

    Wall Township, Southern Regional and Brick Township are admitted.

  7. February 1958

    Jack Arden of Lakewood becomes the first player in New Jersey to score more than 2,000 points in a career, finishing with 2,003.

1960–1969

New sports and 27 schools

Scheduling and alignment took up much of the decade. Soccer, wrestling and tennis were added, and in 1969 the conference settled on four divisions.

John Kolibas, principal of Shore Regional, suggests a sportsmanship award for member schools.

From the conference record, 1967

  1. March 1961

    Soccer is adopted, with a first official schedule in 1962.

  2. March 1962

    Wrestling becomes an official sport.

  3. February 1963

    Bob Verga of St. Rose scores 1,033 points in a season, a Shore and state record.

  4. February 1964

    The first tennis league schedule is approved.

  5. September 1965

    Matawan athletic director Barry Rizzo asks what the conference plans for girls’ sports. Five schools with girls’ programs are already competing with one another.

  6. May 1966

    Asbury Park, which left after the 1961–62 school year, is accepted back.

  7. February to May 1969

    A four-division alignment built on 27 schools passes 23–1. Marlboro, Keansburg and a new Toms River high school officially enter.
